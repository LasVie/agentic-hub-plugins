# Native design

Families and housemates review consumables before a weekly shop or after a confirmed delivery changes. A quick count, estimated daily use and the next routine shopping date suffice to forecast shortage. Dated arrivals, known pack choices and space limits are optional. Purchase options use supplied quantities in one known pack size per item. Product identity and compatibility come from the supplied labels or exact variants. Ambiguous information stays visible for follow-up.

The inventory analyst registers the source and normalizes stock, dates, units and compatibility. In parallel, an independent verifier locks a baseline using the source alone. The leader proposes purchases from the intake receipt using a sequential inventory simulation. After the baseline exists, the verifier checks the proposal using cumulative supply and demand, recalculates the most the shelf can hold, and independently ranks feasible pack choices. Both engines use the same validated source facts. Formal delivery requires a matching original, baseline, proposal and verification receipt.

The projection covers calendar days from the count through the day before the next shop. Eligible arrivals enter before that day's consumption. A current purchase must cover the worst shortage across the period and fit at every modeled opening balance. This assumption makes late confirmed deliveries useful without allowing them to hide earlier shortages. A missing critical value or absent feasible pack defers the decision with a specific reason. Files and receipts remain local. The workflow uses existing AgentGroup tasks and writes to separate new directories.
